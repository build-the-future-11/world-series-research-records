## 5. Mathematical audit

The six-page main paper contains no displayed equations, numbered theorem, proposition or lemma. Its important mathematical claims are prose summaries supported by source/supplement. This is a reproducibility/exposition gap: the following assumptions and derivations should appear in an accessible mathematical appendix. There is no basis to claim that every proposed theorem in the 133-kB blueprint has been validated by this audit.

**WFIM degree truncation and coordinate transport.** Let A be the free associative algebra over a field, with words as basis, and let I be the span of words of length greater than N. Since word lengths add under concatenation, I is a two-sided ideal. The quotient product P_N(xy) is associative. This argument needs integer N≥0 and degree projection, not coefficient-dependent top-k pruning. A top-k operator generally has no ideal property and can destroy associativity; the manuscript correctly avoids extending the guarantee to it.

For invertible diagonal S with S(e_empty)=e_empty, define x⋆y=S⁻¹(Sx Sy). Substitution gives (x⋆y)⋆z=S⁻¹((Sx Sy)Sz)=x⋆(y⋆z); the unit is preserved. If s(uv)=s(u)s(v), every output coefficient factor s(u)s(v)/s(uv) equals one. Thus per-letter scaling cannot change the product. Independent per-word scaling can change coefficients but remains isomorphic, not a new expressive algebra.

The code uses s_w=exp(b tanh θ_w), with empty scale one and finite b>0. Hence e⁻ᵇ≤s_w≤eᵇ and diagonal condition number ≤e²ᵇ. That is a coordinate-map bound, not a bound on every learned decoder or long product's task error. For the encoded unit-plus-letter products, each word feature is multiplied by product(s_letter)/s_word; a fixed diagonal factor can be absorbed by the first unrestricted linear decoder layer. Optimization/regularization may differ. The targeted computation agrees to floating-point precision; it is not the proof itself.

**Implementation counterexamples to blanket exactness:** `wfim/algebra.py:add` deletes coefficients of magnitude below 1e-15. Adding zero to a series with coefficient 1e-16 can therefore erase it. Also `SparseWordSeries(r=-1)` permits a negative “norm” for an odd-degree basis vector; weighted ℓ1 claims require finite r>0. These are real contract limits of legacy sparse code. The dense coordinate-learning path uses a different tensor implementation; no headline prediction is shown to be corrupted. Separate exact algebraic identities from approximate implementation behavior and validate public norm inputs before making library-wide guarantees.

**WFT coarsening.** With P∈R^(2m×m) duplicating group values, R=Pᵀ/2, M_f=I and M_c=2I, L_c=PᵀL_fP and A=M⁻¹L. Congruence alone is insufficient to infer R A_f=A_c R. Equitability/equal cross-group degrees is the required extra assumption; the generator satisfies it. The dimensions on both sides are m×2m. Induction then gives R A_f^k=A_c^k R, and the finite-matrix exponential series yields R exp(-tA_f)=exp(-tA_c)R. A generic weighted graph or arbitrary partition is a counterexample to the missing-assumption version. Fresh checks on both topologies and three sizes support the code's scoped identity.

Positive weighted incidence construction gives symmetric PSD L and zero row sums. A=M⁻¹L is self-adjoint in the mass inner product, not generally the ordinary Euclidean inner product. Mass whitening M⁻¹/² L M⁻¹/² justifies orthogonal spectral coordinates, weighted Parseval and reconstruction. Eigenvectors are nonunique at repeated eigenvalues; complete cluster projectors are the relevant object. Extending rank at near repetitions changes storage. No fixed-rank optimal compression, continuum limit, or FFT complexity theorem is established.

**Concrete WFT prose mismatch:** the protocol calls the generator a cosine “log-weight field,” while `teacher` and `laplacian` use a cosine field passed through softplus. The executable model is w=softplus(Bθ), not w=exp(Bθ). Reflection compatibility survives, but the protocol should state the actual equation and distinguish pre-activation from log weight. This is a specification correction, not evidence that the primary numerical result is false.

**QWIPII correctness.** A finite complete state set, bijective map, fixed goal and preservation of every unit-cost transition justify orbit pruning. On a finite graph, an injective edge map into the same finite edge set is onto; group closure preserves graph distance to the fixed goal. Concrete representative paths retain replayability. The verifier checks full multiset cardinality, duplicates, mapped goals and neighbors; certificate loading revalidates saved mappings. A sample of states or a symmetry transforming the goal into a different goal is insufficient. Weighted or directed extensions require their own cost/edge contract. Greedy ranking is not BFS/shortest-path search despite the legacy function name `ranking_only_bfs`; the manuscript correctly states it can return longer paths.

**WPINN weak form.** For v′=Θ(q,v)ξ and test ψ vanishing at endpoints, integration by parts gives ∫ψΘξ dt=−∫ψ′v dt. The analytic bump `(max(1-s²,0))⁴` has derivative `−8s(max(1-s²,0))³/h` and vanishes at support boundaries. The code's derivative sign and physical-time scale are consistent. Irregular trapezoid weights approximate the integrals; exact weak identities do not imply unbiased noisy quadrature or consistent model recovery. Library terms carry different physical units; coefficient units/RMS scaling must be specified rather than calling raw thresholds comparable across packages.

For q′=v and v′=−kq−(c+dq²+ev²)v, k,c,d,e≥0, E=(v²+kq²)/2 has E′=−cv²−dq²v²−ev⁴≤0. This supports passivity of the restricted continuous model. If k=0 the energy is only semidefinite in position; bounded energy does not prove asymptotic convergence of q. Passivity also does not guarantee that a specified numerical integrator meets its work cap. The four stiff-solver recoveries therefore do not contradict the energy argument or erase original failures.

**Metrics, expectations and differentiability.** QLearn AUC is a trapezoidal sum over normalized query losses including step zero, divided by support-target second moment; it has loss-ratio × step units and is not ROC AUC or simply average per-step loss. QAPEN scores a moment-matched Gaussian, not the exact expert-mixture likelihood. Its NLL conclusion must use that definition. Bootstrap/sign-flip inference is conditional on grouping and symmetry assumptions; it is not a distribution-free claim over all tasks. CWLNN uses a straight-through surrogate for selected rows, not the exact derivative of hard top-k. No convergence theorem for any optimizer/router is stated or established.

## 6. Experimental design, leakage and statistical validity

**Data separation:** QLearn's support/query row handling and whole digit-pair roles are explicit; source duplicate checks passed. Writer partitions and distinct classes do not make one dataset into an unseen dataset population. External Celo2 training overlap remains unknown. WFIM exhausts a small binary set and holds out prefixes/lengths; this is deterministic finite extrapolation, not natural language or memory generalization. QWIPII repeats starts inside four held patterns. WPINN separates whole trajectories but uses only three fixed law parameters, so fresh initial states do not test held laws. CWLNN separates graph IDs and evaluates sizes/topologies/time, while the supplied hierarchy and aligned target deliberately favor hierarchical reasoning.

**Known contamination:** WCODE's four historically seen evaluation families are documented. That invalidates a fresh independent-holdout interpretation, not the observed finite completion rates when labeled developmental. Keep the original endpoint, publish the overlap IDs and any retrospective stratification, and reserve a new unseen evaluation for stronger claims. Do not remove inconvenient families and silently relabel the remainder as the original benchmark.

**Timing/leakage:** QAPEN predicts before observation and tracks later memory utility; the protocol's four-step advance cue is *available input* by design. It is not automatically temporal leakage, but the model's usefulness depends on whether the intended application supplies such information. Retrospective WPINN solver/support interventions and repeated tuning on known optimizer families cannot become fresh confirmation.

**Baselines:** The studies contain meaningful stronger controls and real negative findings. No additional seed cherry-picking, hidden target injection or exact source train/test duplication was found in the reviewed paths. ## 7. Falsification audit

The broad hypothesis “these additions improve their relevant task” is already contradicted for several implementations. That is evidence to preserve, not a reason to tune until it becomes positive.

The most decisive existing falsifiers are: per-letter algebra cancellation; QLearn losing to AdamW/momentum; QAPEN losing to ridge/recent retrieval; QWIPII's extra verification time; WFIM's eight held-length losses; and CWLNN's worse half-routing endpoint. WFT's wrong-symmetry control tests whether an invalid prior can harm, but does not establish robustness to realistic violations of an approximately valid prior. WPINN's wrong-conservation control can remain numerically stable while physically wrong; successful integration is not accurate discovery.

The strongest unresolved alternative explanations are fixed feature reparameterization/conditioning (WFIM), known teacher structure and parameter tying (WFT), prior knowledge and column scaling (WPINN), supplied hierarchy/receptive fields and surrogate training (CWLNN), tiny-domain overhead (QWIPII), candidate-pool containment/development reuse (WCODE), and unmatched meta-training/tuning (QLearn).
